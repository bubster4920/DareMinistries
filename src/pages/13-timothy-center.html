The Timothy Center Initiative.

A safe place for young believers to meet people who want to make a difference in their lives.

Three kinds of people every believer needs.

Every person needs three types of people in their life. The first is a Paul: someone older in the faith who comes alongside you and helps you grow in your walk with Christ. The second is a Barnabas: a peer who walks with you through life. The last is a Timothy: someone younger in the faith whom God has placed in your life to encourage and bring up.

In years of ministry we have seen plenty of Barnabases, but a huge shortage of Pauls and Timothys. The Timothy Center is a recognition that there is a void in the intergenerational gap, and we are seeking to fill it.

What we hope to build

A place to assemble, express, build and grow.

A space where students can simply hang out, express their interests, whether art or gaming, and use those interests as a way to know them better. Relationships lead to life-changing conversations.

Character, value and a lifetime of ministry.

We want to help young people build character and learn to reach their peers with the good news of Christ through something as simple as kindness, valuing others, themselves and life itself.

Why relationships.

El Paso County, Colorado has one of the highest teen suicide rates in the nation, and our community is still learning how to address it. One of the key missing pieces is relationships: students need friends and mentors who know them and invest in their lives.

Most of all, we hope the Timothy Center helps people grow: in their abilities, in their spiritual walk, and in their ability to make disciples. We want to give value to broken and hurting youth, prepare young Saints for a lifetime of ministry, and unify the body of Christ.

The first step: The Upper Room.

In the summer of 2019, Dare took the first steps of the Initiative by opening a coffee house called The Upper Room in Colorado Springs, in the heart of the UCCS neighborhood. In partnership with Cragmor CRC, it ran as a donation-based coffee house staffed entirely by volunteers, with the goal of reaching students who live nearby.

Our hope was that it would grow into a space able to sustain staff, host counselors, and reach the community and the university. Volunteers gave two hours of their week to keep the space safe and welcoming: being present, with freedom to work on their own things while they were there.

We prayed that the Lord would bless the endeavor and use it to change lives.
